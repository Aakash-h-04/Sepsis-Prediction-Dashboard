The 4 Stages of Sepsis Pathophysiology

Click on any stage to inspect the underlying cellular and vascular mechanisms.

STAGE 01

Primary Infection

Bacteria, viruses, or fungi enter the lungs, abdomen, urinary tract, or skin, releasing PAMPs.

STAGE 02

Host Hyper-Inflammation

Macrophage activation triggers a pro-inflammatory storm (TNF-α, IL-1β, IL-6).

STAGE 03

Microvascular Breakdown

Endothelial permeability increases; capillary leak leads to intravascular volume depletion.

STAGE 04

Multiorgan Dysfunction

Cellular hypoxia, microthrombi, and mitochondrial exhaustion culminate in organ failure (MODS).

Stage 1: Primary Infection & Pathogen Recognition

The process begins when pathogens breach physical barriers. Common origins include pneumonia (lungs), intra-abdominal infections, urinary tract infections (UTIs), or catheter entry. Microbial components known as Pathogen-Associated Molecular Patterns (PAMPs) such as lipopolysaccharide (LPS) bind to Toll-like Receptors (TLRs) on resident immune cells, initiating the immune cascade.

Physiological Correlate: Normal to elevated resting heart rate, mild temperature fluctuation as the hypothalamus responds to pyrogens.

Innate vs. Adaptive Immune Dysregulation

Early sepsis is characterized by an excessive innate inflammatory burst (cytokines like TNF-alpha and IL-1). Simultaneously or shortly thereafter, extensive apoptosis of lymphocytes occurs, plunging the patient into profound compensatory anti-inflammatory and immunosuppressive paralysis (sepsis-induced immunoparalysis).

Clinical Terminology Glossary

Search clinical terms used in ICU sepsis assessment:

SIRS (Systemic Inflammatory Response Syndrome)

Clinical criteria including fever/hypothermia, tachycardia, tachypnea, and leukocytosis. Sensitive but non-specific.

SOFA / qSOFA Score

Sequential Organ Failure Assessment. Measures organ deterioration across respiratory, cardiovascular, hepatic, coagulation, renal, and neurological systems.

Serum Lactate (> 2 mmol/L)

Biomarker of anaerobic metabolism caused by microcirculatory hypoperfusion and cellular hypoxia.

DIC (Disseminated Intravascular Coagulation)

Pathological systemic activation of blood coagulation resulting in microthrombi that block capillary blood flow while consuming platelets.
