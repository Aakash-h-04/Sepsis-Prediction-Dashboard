Critical Design Rationale: Threshold Tuning for Asymmetric Risk

Why Not Use the 0.50 Standard Threshold?

In medical datasets where sepsis occurs in only ~2% of observations, standard 0.50 probability cutoffs produce near-zero sensitivity (the model predicts class 0 for almost everything to maximize raw accuracy). Shifting the threshold to 0.01 optimizes recall to 55.3%, prioritizing patient safety over false alarm suppression.

Why Is Patient-Level Splitting Critical?

ICU telemetry contains hundreds of sequential hourly readings for each patient. Splitting randomly on individual rows creates extreme data leakage—a model memorizes a patient's baseline vital signs from the training split and artificially inflates test metrics. Group-splitting on PatientID ensures that whole patient trajectories remain isolated in the test set.
