Resuscitation Kinetics

The Mortality Cost of Delayed Treatment

Groundbreaking data from Kumar et al. (Critical Care Medicine) demonstrating the exponential rise in mortality per hour of antimicrobial delay during septic shock:

Hour 0–1 (Golden Hour) 79.9% Survival
Hour 2 Delay 70.5% Survival
Hour 4 Delay 52.1% Survival
Hour 6+ Delay 42.0% Survival
"Each hour of delay in antimicrobial administration over the first 6 hours after onset of septic shock-associated hypotension produces an average 7.6% decrease in survival."
Geographic Inequality

Disproportionate Burden Across Regions

Sepsis prevalence is heavily modulated by healthcare infrastructure, sanitation, and neonatal resources:

Sub-Saharan Africa & South Asia

Account for over 65% of global sepsis incidence and >70% of pediatric sepsis deaths, driven by maternal sepsis, diarrheal disease, and respiratory infections.

Latin America & Eastern Europe

Intermediate incidence with hospital-acquired antimicrobial-resistant (AMR) pathogens posing increasing therapeutic barriers.

High-Income Countries (HICs)

Sepsis predominantly affects geriatric patients with multiple comorbidities and chronic organ failure, representing the most costly condition in US hospital care (>$24 billion/yr).
