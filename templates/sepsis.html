CLINICAL INTELLIGENCE & PATHOPHYSIOLOGY

When the immune system turns against itself.

Sepsis is not a simple infection. It is a life-threatening, dysregulated host response that causes tissue damage, organ hypoperfusion, and septic shock.

🚨
Critical Clinical Alert: Sepsis is a medical emergency requiring rapid hospital intervention. Every hour of delay increases mortality risk by 7.6%. If sepsis is suspected, dial 112 / 911 / 999 immediately.
Mechanism

The Dysregulated Cascade

How a localized pathogen escalates into systemic multiorgan dysfunction:

1. Infection & Pathogen Recognition

PAMPs (Pathogen-Associated Molecular Patterns) trigger innate toll-like receptors (TLRs) on immune cells.

2. Cytokine Storm & Inflammation

Massive release of pro-inflammatory mediators (TNF-α, IL-1β, IL-6) overrides compensatory anti-inflammatory signals.

3. Endothelial Degradation & Capillary Leak

The endothelial glycocalyx degrades, allowing plasma to leak into interstitial tissues, triggering profound arterial hypotension.

4. Microvascular Thrombosis & Organ Hypoxia

Platelet activation triggers disseminated intravascular microclots, depriving vital organs of oxygen.

Epidemiology

High-Risk Patient Populations

Anyone can develop sepsis, but specific groups face drastically heightened vulnerability:

Neonates & Children under 5
Immature innate and adaptive immune responses
40% Global Cases
Adults Age 65 and Older
Immunosenescence, polypharmacy, and co-morbidities
High Mortality
Immunocompromised Patients
Chemotherapy, organ transplant, HIV/AIDS, steroids
Critical Risk
Chronic Organ Disease
Cirrhosis, chronic kidney disease (CKD), diabetes, COPD
Reduced Reserve
Clinical Etiology

Primary Infection Origins

Distribution of primary anatomical infection sites precipitating sepsis:

🫁 Respiratory Tract (Pneumonia / Lung Infection) ~50%
🫘 Genitourinary Tract (UTI / Pyelonephritis) ~20%
🩺 Intra-Abdominal (Peritonitis / Appendicitis) ~15%
🩹 Skin & Soft Tissue (Cellulitis / Wound Infection) ~10%
Emergency Resuscitation

The Golden Hour: Sepsis Six Protocol

The internationally recognized resuscitation bundle to be initiated within 60 minutes:

1. High-Flow Oxygen

Titrate to target SpO₂ 94–98% (or 88–92% in COPD).

2. Blood Cultures

Draw microbiological cultures before antimicrobial therapy.

3. IV Antibiotics

Administer broad-spectrum intravenous antimicrobials.

4. IV Fluid Resuscitation

Rapid crystalloid bolus (30 mL/kg) for hypotension or lactate ≥4.

5. Serum Lactate

Check arterial or venous lactate to detect tissue hypoperfusion.

6. Strict Urine Output

Catheterize to monitor renal perfusion (>0.5 mL/kg/h).
