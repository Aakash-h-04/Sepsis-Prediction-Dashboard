PUBLIC AWARENESS

Sepsis is a medical emergency. Understanding it matters.

Learn the basics, recognize why timely medical assessment is important, and explore trusted public-health resources.

!
If sepsis is suspected, seek urgent medical care.

Do not wait for an online tool, score, or chatbot to decide whether someone needs help.

What is sepsis?

Sepsis is a life-threatening condition that occurs when the body's response to an infection causes injury to its own tissues and organs. It can progress quickly and needs prompt medical assessment and treatment.

How can it develop?

An infection can trigger a dysregulated response that affects circulation, blood vessels, clotting, and organ function. The course varies across people and infections; no single symptom or vital sign can establish or exclude sepsis.

Who may be at greater risk?

Risk depends on factors such as age, underlying health conditions, immune status, recent severe illness or procedures, and the nature of the infection. Risk factors are not a diagnosis, and sepsis can affect people without an obvious pre-existing risk factor.

What should people know?

  • Sepsis can follow different types of infection.
  • Warning signs may include confusion, difficulty breathing, severe discomfort, clammy skin, or feeling extremely unwell.
  • Symptoms vary; a list is not a reliable rule-out test.
  • Preventing infections, keeping recommended vaccinations up to date, wound care, and getting appropriate care for infections can reduce some risks.
